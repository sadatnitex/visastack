# Answer From Your Records: commercial notes

Written 12 Nov 2026. Everything here is reasoning from the product as built, not from payment data.
There is no payment evidence for this capability, and production holds made-up records only.

## Who would pay

Agencies, not travellers. An agency's cost in a visa case is chasing: asking, reminding, receiving
the wrong or an old file, asking again. A traveller who already holds a checked record and can answer
a whole ask in one confirmed step costs that agency less staff time per case and gets to a complete
file sooner. The traveller's side of this step should stay free: it is what makes the traveller
bring their record to the next agency.

## Urgency

High at the moment a case starts (the first ask is the largest) and before a deadline. Low between
cases. So the value recurs per case, not per month: it supports a per-case or per-seat agency plan
better than a traveller subscription.

## The distribution loop

1. Agency A asks. The traveller answers and A reviews: the record now holds checked, dated copies,
   some of them accepted.
2. Agency B, unrelated, asks. The traveller sees B's name, what B asks, what is already held, and
   sends it in one confirmed step.
3. B receives a complete, exact answer faster than from a traveller who starts again. That is B's
   reason to keep asking through VisaStack and to invite its other clients.

The step built here is link 2. Links 1 and 3 already existed.

## Expected operating benefit for agencies (hypotheses, not measurements)

- Fewer reminders per case, because the traveller's effort to answer a multi-item ask drops from one
  loop per item to one review.
- Fewer wrong files, because an expired or over-age copy is held out of the one-step send and the
  date is shown beside every copy.
- Not claimed: fewer review decisions. The agency still decides on every copy, one by one.

## What stays free, always

Seeing who asks and what they ask; choosing the exact copy; the receipt of what was sent; ending an
agency's access; the history of sends and decisions. These are the traveller's trust and privacy
rights, not features to sell.

## Cheapest test of willingness to pay

With two or three pilot agencies and made-up or consented records: measure the time from an ask of
five or more documents to a complete answer, for travellers who answer one by one against travellers
who use the review step, and ask each agency one question afterwards: "would you pay per case for
clients who arrive like this?" No billing work is needed for that test.

## A caution

Sending several answers at once looks strong in a demonstration. Its recurring value depends on how
often an ask has several documents the traveller already holds, which only real use can show. The
parts with certain recurring value are smaller: the agency's name before a send, and the date beside
each copy.
