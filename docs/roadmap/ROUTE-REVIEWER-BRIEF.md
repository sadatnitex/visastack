# Route checklist: brief for the qualified reviewer

Prepared 5 Nov 2026 for the owner to hand to an immigration adviser. VisaStack has written no route checklist on purpose: a checklist tells a traveller what to gather, and it must come from a person who answers for it.

## What you are asked to do

Write, or check, one checklist for one route (for example Bangladesh to UK Standard Visitor) from current official sources, and sign it. You are not asked to give advice to any traveller. A checklist states what the authority asks for. It never says a person will be approved.

## What each checklist holds

| Field | What to write |
|---|---|
| Route | Country the person travels to, the visa type, and the nationality or residence it applies to |
| Requirement | One thing to gather, in plain words (for example "Passport valid for the whole stay") |
| Why it is asked | One sentence, in the authority's own sense |
| Required or optional | Whether the authority requires it for this route |
| What counts | The kind of document accepted, and any rule on age, language, translation or validity |
| Source | The official page it comes from (link) and the date you read it |
| Notes for the agency | Anything an agency must check that a traveller cannot |

## Rules for the text

- Plain words, no jargon. A traveller reads it on a phone.
- One requirement per line. No requirement longer than 30 words.
- State only what the official source says. If a point comes from practice and not from the source, leave it out or label it "Practice, not an official rule".
- Never write a promise, a chance, a score or a "will be approved".

## What happens after you sign

The owner approves the checklist in the pack studio, it is published, and cases on that route start showing it. A checklist past its review date stays usable and shows "due for review".
